## Blind take — recorded before reading the change design or tasks

1. Pin the exact party hostname as WebAuthn rp.id; ports and display names must never define separate credential scopes.
2. Pin operator-registry resolution, canonical hostname/origin handling, and rejection of unknown or suspended parties before both ceremony and issuance.
3. Pin discoverable registration with residentKey=required and authenticatorAttachment unset so synced password managers remain valid holders.
4. Refuse any path from a WebAuthn assertion or party certificate directly into the OIDC URN grant; that grant still verifies the IdentiKey Response and XID binding.
5. Pin the holder key, proof of its possession, and the registration-to-issuance association; a passkey account is not automatically an IdentiKey signing key.
6. Pin an acyclic dual-certificate construction with exact hash preimages, signature coverage, and paired verification so neither certificate can be substituted.
7. Pin deterministic dCBOR bytes, version/type/domain separation, raw byte fields, algorithm-aware fingerprints, and explicit text encodings.
8. Pin a path to the configured local group anchor, with intermediate authority and scope constraints; sharing a hostname suffix alone must confer no trust.
9. Refuse a present boundary slot in v1 and refuse any implication that party membership establishes keyspace membership or agency authority.
10. Accept the operational cost of per-party DNS/TLS and passkey enrollment in return for isolation, provided certificate lifecycle and failure semantics are explicit enough to implement.

## Verdict and comparison

Send back for contract completion before act. The human decisions are preserved in direction, but independent implementations cannot yet agree on certificate decoding, holder authorization, and accepted paths. This is a review of the proposed standard, not a demand to implement the OP or CA here.

The strongest reading of the author's design answers much of the blind take: registry-gated full-host rp.id, discoverable credentials without an attachment restriction, raw dCBOR byte fields, the existing algorithm-aware fingerprint, and a configured local anchor are all appropriate. The explicit WebAuthn rejection remains in the delta, and keyspaces remain a separate object. A single issuer and a narrow first profile can be sufficient; neither arbitrary parent trust nor a reputation evaluator is needed.

### R1 — High: finish the X.509 profile and make boundary rejection executable

Evidence: `design.md:103-115,143-161,186-204`; `specs/identikey-party/spec.md:87-102,110-114`; `tasks.md:5`.

The design promises identical claims in both encodings, but specifies only a SAN URI and a twin digest on the X.509 side. There is no defined location or decoding rule for the WebAuthn credential id, and no precise encoding for the group/issuer fingerprint. The twin digest commits indirectly to those fields, but cannot reveal them to an X.509-only verifier. The statement that either encoding can be used with the twin required only by policy leaves those verification semantics unresolved.

Specify the mapping for every claim, including the subject-to-SPKI conversion and validity equality. If some claims are intentionally available only through the committed dCBOR twin, say so and require that twin whenever those claims are used. Pin each custom extension's OID, value syntax, criticality, and duplicate/malformed handling. A digest extension needs an unambiguous encoded value, not just the phrase “SHA-256.”

The boundary extension has no OID at all. A v1 verifier cannot reject a known reserved extension it cannot identify. Unknown non-critical X.509 extensions can be ignored, so generic path validation does not supply this protection; the profile must check its reserved boundary OID regardless of criticality. Explicitly cover host, holder, intermediate, and configured-anchor handling. These requirements follow from the extension model in [RFC 5280 §4.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5280.html#section-4.2).

Acceptance evidence: a field/extension mapping and negative fixture hooks for missing required twin, mismatched claims, and boundary presence with either criticality. Selecting numbers during act is reasonable only if the documented OID authority and all required slots are identified; the current task mentions only the twin-hash OID.

### R2 — High: bind enrollment to the subject IdentiKey key

Evidence: `design.md:92-109,165-178`; `specs/identikey-party/spec.md:87-94`.

Successful passkey registration is the sole stated issuance prerequisite, while the certificate carries a separate IdentiKey public key. No rule establishes where that key comes from, who may select it, or how it is bound to the same holder and enrollment transaction. An implementation could accept a supplied public key belonging to someone else and certify an unsupported holder/key association. A valid passkey registration alone does not answer this question.

Specify an authenticated enrollment binding covering the holder/account, party/rp.id, credential id, subject key, and fresh transaction. Require proof of possession of the subject key or a defined managed-custody authorization equivalent, and prevent replay or replacement of the subject after authorization. Recheck active party status at issuance. The contract may name existing primitives; no new ceremony routes or custody implementation are owed here.

Acceptance evidence: hooks rejecting a substituted subject key, another enrollment's credential id, replay, and suspension between ceremony and issuance. Keep this proof separate from granting OAuth tokens.

### R3 — High: define legal paths and separate group identity from signer identity where necessary

Evidence: `design.md:61-75,95-105,123,180-190`; `specs/identikey-party/spec.md:59-72,99`.

Stopping at configured G is correct and honors the local-trust decision. Requiring that a parent P not gain authority is also correct. The unresolved part is what is allowed below G: the delta accepts a chain ending at G, while the object defines `group` as the immediate issuer's fingerprint and requires its signature under the group key. For leaf → I → G, those readings disagree: does `group` identify I or G, and which key verifies the dCBOR signature? The only scenario, leaf → G → P, does not resolve this.

State the allowed v1 path shapes. A direct-issuer-only v1 profile is a defensible bounded choice if intermediates below G are explicitly rejected and the hierarchy relationship above G is described without granting it trust. If delegated issuers below G are supported, identify the group anchor separately from the immediate signer, define how signer authority is validated, and require appropriate CA constraints, key usage, and path limits. Clarify how the self-signed G requirement relates to a parent-signed representation of that same group key.

A path ending at an anchor is not by itself a complete validation rule. Reference the applicable X.509 path checks and add the party-specific checks. Explicitly inspect the configured anchor for the reserved boundary rather than assuming ordinary path validation does so: a self-signed trust-anchor certificate is outside the prospective path in [RFC 5280 §6.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5280.html#section-6.1).

Acceptance evidence: accepted/rejected outcomes for leaf → G, leaf → I → G, an unrelated root, and parent-only trust, with the expected group id and signing key stated.

### R4 — Medium: specify how the same HTTPS entry serves different trust configurations

Evidence: `design.md:77-88`; `specs/identikey-party/spec.md:74-85`.

The design says the same hostname offers a group-issued server chain to group members and a publicly trusted chain to browsers without the group CA. It does not define how the deployment chooses a chain or how a group verifier obtains and validates the group-issued entry when the live TLS connection presents the public chain. Both clients can request the same hostname; the hostname alone cannot select between these outcomes.

Pin a supported deployment contract: the trust provisioning or certificate-selection mechanism, and which observed certificate proves group entry. Do not silently treat a publicly trusted certificate as proof of membership in the configured local group. This is a missing contract, not a request to write TLS routing code.

Acceptance evidence: describe one ordinary browser connection and one configured group-verifier connection to the production origin, including the chain each receives and what each establishes.

## Dual-hash judgment

The normative five-step construction at `design.md:151-161` is coherent. Let C0 be canonical dCBOR(PartyCert) with the twin hash zeroed, X be the final signed DER containing SHA-256(C0), and C be the final PartyCert containing SHA-256(X). Sign C last. X commits to the normalized claims; signed C commits to the exact X. No fixed point is required. The reverse commitment is not a hash of the finished PartyCertSig wrapper or its signature, and the standard must say that precisely.

The preceding prose at `design.md:99-100,143-148` instead claims hashes of each other's final bytes and offers a placeholder/alternate order. Delete that competing explanation and retain one algorithm. Define strict canonical decoding, the exact normalized map, and paired verification of both signatures, both hashes, semantic equality, validity, and issuer authorization. Name substitution and non-canonical-input fixture hooks; filled signature vectors remain outside the authorized scope. This is a documentation correction to a sound construction, not a finding that the five-step order is circular.

## Remaining observations

- The grant and keyspace boundaries are not collapsed. Preserve the URN grant's existing XID-current-key and pairwise-sub requirements as well as signature verification; party certification is not a substitute for them (`identikey-oidc-urn-grant-v1.md:97-111`).
- Pin exact expected-origin verification in addition to rp.id. The design itself correctly notes that dev ports distinguish origins even though they share rp.id. The password-manager scenario remains an empirical task, not evidence already obtained.
- The issuer signs a bare versioned map without a party-specific type/context. Prefer a fixed party signing domain, or explicitly justify and constrain this use of the shared CA key. Do not change the established auth-challenge payload to achieve it.
- The lifetime recommendation can remain simple: suspension blocks new ceremonies and issuance; offline acceptance of an already issued certificate lasts until the profile's stated expiry/revocation rule. Make the online-status failure behavior explicit if online status becomes normative.
- `design.md:211-212` cannot copy a single normative skew value from auth-challenge: that spec permits a tolerance, and `crates/identikey-auth/src/verify.rs:32-53` takes `skew_secs` as an argument. Choose the party policy explicitly.
