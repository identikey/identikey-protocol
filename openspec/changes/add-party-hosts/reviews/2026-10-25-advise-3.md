## Blind pass

1. Pin each operator-registered party hostname to its own WebAuthn RP ID and explicit endpoint identity.
2. Pin public-key algorithm and canonical raw key bytes together; identity fingerprints must commit to both through dCBOR.
3. Require a holder certificate's subject to identify the actual enrolled key, with possession established before issuance.
4. Bind enrollment proof to a server-owned pending operation, fresh nonce, intended party, and fixed expiry.
5. Require dCBOR and X.509 certificate representations to agree on holder identity and validity at an explicit precision.
6. Specify deterministic cross-representation commitments with an acyclic construction and verifiable byte boundaries.
7. Pin the local-group anchor's authority and distinguish it from browser-facing HTTPS authentication.
8. Refuse any substitution of a WebAuthn assertion for the IdentiKey OIDC URN grant and its XID-binding checks.
9. Preserve discoverable access-passkey registration and support synced authenticators without forcing platform attachment.
10. Accept the interoperability cost of dual certificates only with precise encoding rules and fail-closed mismatch handling.

## R2 follow-up: resolved

`design.md:180–193` fixes the ordering: the authenticated holder submits a subject key, the endpoint stores it immutably as K1 on the pending enrollment, and only then issues the nonce. The nonce binds K1 together with the party, RP ID, origin, and credential ID. The endpoint stores the deadline, and the signed `exp` must equal it; both larger and smaller values fail.

The issuance rules at `design.md:213–227` independently require equality with the pending subject and signature verification under K1. A first submission naming K2 with a valid K2 signature therefore fails even before replay becomes relevant. Checking the stored deadline under the stated skew cannot be bypassed by changing the signed expiry.

The delta at `specs/identikey-party/spec.md:135–171` makes the same ordering, immutability, subject equality, and deadline equality normative. Its hooks cover a matching K1 first submission, first-submission K2 substitution, extended expiry, and replay. Exact equality also rejects shortened expiry without requiring another scenario. The successful-submission hook deliberately says the subject check passes, so it does not waive the remaining issuance checks.

## R1 follow-up: resolved

`design.md:288–293` explicitly requires whole-second Zulu validity with the same Unix-second meanings as `nbf` and `exp`, using UTCTime before `2050-01-01T00:00:00Z` and GeneralizedTime at and after that instant. The rule applies to each validity value independently, including a certificate that spans the boundary.

`specs/identikey-party/spec.md:187–194` states the same requirement. Its present-day and transition hooks at lines 219–229 cover both UTCTime values and a mixed-tag certificate from `2049-12-31T23:59:59Z` to `2050-01-01T00:00:00Z`. Unix-second equality remains mandatory. The design expressly preserves C0 → X → C, and its construction at lines 308–334 still binds the final signed DER, including the actual time tags. No competing hash construction is introduced.

## Comparison and steelman

The ten-line blind take above was persisted before opening the design, tasks, delta, or earlier review. It is a set of concerns to answer, not a demand for a different architecture. The strongest reading of this contract answers them: an operator admits a party hostname; WebAuthn authenticates the enrollment session; the holder chooses and proves possession of a separately pinned IdentiKey subject; and G issues a mandatory pair of certificates with explicit claim equality and byte commitments. Hostname partitioning, discoverable synced passkeys, algorithm-committing fingerprints, and the separate OIDC URN grant remain consistent with the cited living specs.

Pinning the holder's selection after the ceremony and before the nonce is sufficient for the stated enrollment model. It does not require moving IdentiKey subject selection into WebAuthn registration or introducing an additional identity authorization system. Date-dependent ASN.1 tags likewise answer the interoperability concern without redesigning the certificate pair: the signed map commits to the final DER regardless of which required time tag appears.

The new text does not break the previously resolved direct path to configured G (R3), the separate public handshake and published group-entry certificate (R4), or the dual-hash order. Those findings remain resolved and are not reopened. The cost of two mandatory certificate representations is justified here by the now explicit identity, validity, and commitment checks.
