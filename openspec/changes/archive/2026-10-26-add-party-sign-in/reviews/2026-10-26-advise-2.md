## Blind pass

Written in the review session before opening design.md or tasks.md, from the packet Why, living `docs/standards/identikey-party-v1.md` §§4, 5, and 11, and the 2026-10-25 entry in `docs/LEARNINGS.md`. Reproduced here unchanged.

1. Pin sign-in to `credentials.get` for the party hostname; ports do not distinguish relying parties.
2. Keep `credentials.create` behind a separate, explicit registration control.
3. Require a fresh challenge and verified assertion before establishing a session.
4. Treat database acceptance as necessary alongside cryptographic verification.
5. Bind the credential and resulting session to the correct holder and party.
6. Require an authorized holder session before attaching another passkey.
7. Preserve stable, opaque `user.id`; refuse guarantees about multiple credentials surviving in one vault.
8. State replacement behavior without treating vault contents as database authority.
9. Keep enrollment proof, certificate issuance, and OIDC grant authorization separate from passkey sign-in.
10. Tradeoff: vault reconciliation can improve usability, but must remain optional and unable to invalidate successful sign-in.

## Comparison and steelman

The revised design answers these concerns without changing the holder model or expanding this standards amendment into a database or recovery implementation. An authorized session gates attachment, a verified assertion establishes sign-in, and a vault signal follows that success. Enrollment retains its independent K1 proof and nonce; the passkey remains insufficient for the OIDC URN grant.

The strongest argument against my initial concern about replacement is that retaining old database rows need not imply retaining old vault credentials. The author now makes that distinction explicit: another authenticator may still possess an accepted credential, so registration alone is not grounds for deleting its row. A stable handle also keeps holder lookup and signal grouping coherent. That resolves the concern without requiring a new handle scheme or promising two surviving passkeys in one vault.

Likewise, incorporating WebAuthn verification avoids restating the entire external standard. The shorter delta names WebAuthn assertion verification; the accompanying design identifies the Level 3 operation and explicitly enumerates the previously missing checks. I assess the named two-document contract as a whole, rather than reading the delta's shorter list as permission to omit checks stated in the design.
