## Independent blind take

1. Pin a stable collective identity and an authenticated genesis without granting a retained founder key permanent authority.
2. Pin how a founding-hash identity preserves future lineage replacement; explicitly amend the core identity/binding contract where necessary.
3. Count distinct prior-state principals, and make changes to voting eligibility as protected as the powers they can acquire.
4. Require incoming control consent and reject key aliasing that lets one credential masquerade as multiple principals.
5. Keep authorization, settlement, activation, freshness, and one-use consumption distinct, even when one record proves several facts.
6. Refuse a cached head as sufficient evidence to install a new durable external binding after a revocation or handoff.
7. Pin witness replacement, conflicting settlement, persistent freeze behavior, and the precise availability assumptions of recovery.
8. Pin detached grant scope, audience, expiry, revocation, and policy-handoff treatment independently of roster membership.
9. Specify exact canonical bytes, digest domains, signature transcripts, and rejection rules; the log actor verifier is not a quorum verifier.
10. Trade general governance mechanisms and secret release for a small public-log profile, provided its limits and ADR departures are explicit.

Blind take persisted before opening design.md, tasks.md, steer.md, or notes/. The banner records the final verdict.

## Verdict and comparison

**Send back.** The founding commitment, prior-state principal counting, role/key guard on roster edits, detached grants, and separation of approval from settlement are good foundations. I would not yet let an implementer write the standard from this contract. The following are my independently written findings, recorded before reading the author's self-critique.

The hash-derived XID is a sound answer to authenticating genesis from one configured identifier. It prevents a retained inception key from manufacturing an alternative genesis for that identifier. It does not authenticate *currentness*: an old authorized witness can still answer about an old prefix. The motivation should distinguish these two problems. ADR-013 decision 1 itself states the distinct identity/lineage requirement, not the detailed inception-key construction attributed to it here; the accepted change is a deliberate replacement of the identity/binding model, not merely a different hash function.

## Required changes

### R1 — Ordered consumption is missing for a new durable binding

**Classification:** contract mismatch with ADR-013; safety defect if presented as equivalent protection.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Head and freshness” and “Forgejo CI ownership”; delta requirements “Head freshness and no rollback” and “Grants are detached and delegation comes first”.

Counterexample: grant G is settled; a consumer requests nonce N and receives a head containing G; revocation or a handoff voiding G settles next; the consumer then installs the durable binding using N for the first time. Its stored head has not advanced, N matches and is unspent, and the signed head can still be inside `head_age`. All stated consumer checks pass. ADR-013's living scenario “Handoff beats an outstanding action” requires refusal. “Partition before consume” also fails: the consumer can lose contact after reading and still install. A second consumer with its own nonce can repeat installation; grant issuance never consumed either consumer's operation.

The delegate entry can be the ordered issuance of a grant. It cannot by itself be a consumer-specific, single-use admission for a later external action. Decision 4 expressly says a settlement record alone is not enough for secret release. Secret release is out of scope, so do not claim this wire implements that decision. Ordinary delegated uses may deliberately accept bounded staleness, but the living governance contract and existing Forgejo profile impose a stronger boundary for these installations.

**Satisfaction:** define an action/consumer-bound durable consume ordered with revocation and handoff, including retries and replay; or explicitly narrow v1 to bounded-stale delegation and make the stronger installation protocol a prerequisite to the Forgejo integration. Remove the claim that the present nonce head is sufficient. Add the read–handoff–install, partition-before-consume, consume-before-handoff, and second-consumer scenarios. Also specify future-clock rejection/skew and nonce lifetime: currently a future-dated head has no upper time bound and an outstanding install nonce has no explicit expiry.

### R2 — Policy replacement bypasses the advertised per-rule protection

**Classification:** safety defect in the stated invariant; authorization ambiguity.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Authorization” and “Role and key guard”; delta “Quorum authorization per action” and “Role and key changes need the rules they affect”.

Example: `policy = 1 of admin`, `roster = 1 of admin`, `delegate = 2 of owner`. One admin cannot mint owners through a roster edit, but can validly replace the policy with `delegate = 1 of admin` and then delegate alone. It can also remove the old delegate rule, change the unprotected old role's membership, then restore the rule. Thus “To gain the power to pass a rule, the group must already pass that rule” is false for the accepted policy-edit path. The roster guard alone is sound for a fixed policy; it is not an invariant across all valid entries.

A policy rule as an explicit constitutional override is a defensible choice. It must be stated as such, including that a high threshold on another action is subordinate to the policy rule. If the intended invariant really protects each action against all below-threshold coalitions, policy edits must protect the affected rules too, including deletion/reintroduction and role changes.

**Satisfaction:** choose and state the hierarchy, constrain configurations if necessary, and add the exact multi-entry example above as an accept or reject scenario. Reconcile the unconditional guard claim with that result. Do not silently leave implementers to choose between an override and independent protected powers.

### R3 — Policy changes can confer unaccepted governing control

**Classification:** safety defect relative to the incoming-consent guarantee; ambiguity between design and delta.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Acceptance”; delta “Incoming control signs”.

A roster quorum adds X with only unused role `future-owner` and arbitrary keys, without any acceptance (expressly allowed). A later policy entry names `future-owner` as its policy role. The design expressly exempts this operation from acceptance. X now governs although X and its keys never signed anything. Admission by another quorum does not prove consent or possession. This also lets a quorum install unusable keys despite the stated “no key counts ... before it has been shown to work” guarantee. The delta's general sentence about gaining a governing role does not clearly include or exclude this path.

**Satisfaction:** define acceptance across policy-induced activation of roles, including existing unproven keys, and require the relevant new control evidence; alternatively explicitly revise the consent guarantee and obtain agreement to that exception. Add a policy-promotes-unused-role scenario and one where an old unproven secondary key first becomes an approval key.

### R4 — Replay is not a complete deterministic state machine

**Classification:** ambiguity.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Log”, “Grants”, and “Transport”; delta “Every entry commits to its resulting state” and grant requirements.

`State.grants` is called “live grant digests”, but grant bodies are detached and may never be available to the replaying verifier. Actual liveness depends on body timestamps and current time. Historical state digests cannot depend on the replay clock or possession of those bodies. Distinguish structurally active digests from grants usable now.

Several transitions remain undefined: a member appearing in both `set` and `drop`; duplicate XIDs or duplicate set entries; duplicate roles/keys/digests; revoke of an unknown or already revoked digest; re-delegation of a revoked or policy-voided digest; `keep` containing a never-issued or previously voided digest. For example, replacing the active set with `keep` resurrects a voided digest, while intersecting with the prior active set does not. The prose liveness predicate forbids resurrection across an unkept policy change, but no reducer pins how that fact is represented or checked. Retaining only the advertised state is not necessarily sufficient to resume verification.

**Satisfaction:** specify the reducer for every kind, no-op and duplicate semantics, and whether any tombstone/history is needed. Define `State.grants` independent of wall time and detached-body availability. Pin unique ordering or preservation rules for roles, set/drop/keep arrays and all state arrays; dCBOR does not sort arrays. Specify all byte lengths (including grant salt), integer bounds, malformed windows, optional-empty equivalence, nested unknown-field handling, and bundle order/completeness. Add deterministic replay scenarios with absent bodies and different clocks, conflicting roster edits, and each grant resurrection path.

### R5 — The signature transcript is misstated and the verification profile incomplete

**Classification:** ambiguity with an existing-code contract mismatch.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Log”, “Authorization”, H1; delta authorization and chain requirements.

The design says attached signatures are “over a stable content hash”. In `crates/identikey-log/src/sign.rs`, `Author::raw_sign` signs `unsigned.wrap().digest()` (the Gordian SHA-256 digest tree). `codec::content_hash` is Blake3 of canonical unsigned envelope bytes. These are different messages. `verify` checks every signature against `Op.actor`; `counter_sign` can attach other keys' signatures but does not supply a quorum verifier. The standard must say which digest to reconstruct, how signatures are matched to prior and incoming keys, and how many times a principal can count. Strict Ed25519 is specified for receipts but not explicitly for member approvals/acceptance; weak-key rejection is also missing, unlike the Forgejo profile.

The underlying codec permits elision, and documents that reconstructing an elided op changes its content hash. A self-verifying hash-linked group bundle needs an explicit rule for elided/salted envelopes and optional log assertions. Also pin whether the proposer must actually sign; “must be a member” alone allows an unsigned actor attribution. The behavior for malformed, invalid, unsupported-algorithm and unmatched signatures is not fully specified by “outside the roster do not count and do not invalidate”.

**Satisfaction:** define the exact signed bytes and envelope constraints, strict signature/key validation, signature matching/counting, acceptance keys, proposer proof and treatment of extraneous evidence. Add transcript-oracle hooks distinguishing the two hashes, non-actor co-signatures, duplicate-device signatures, invalid/weak signatures, and elided evidence. H1 should validate this actual contract, not a signature over `content_hash`.

### R6 — Witness exit, freshness trust and freeze semantics overclaim availability

**Classification:** ambiguity and safety/availability defect in the stated guarantees.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Settlement”, “Exit witness”, “Head and freshness”; delta receipt, exit and head requirements.

The exit path works when the current witness refuses and no competing valid entry has been settled. It is not an unconditional escape: owners may have previously approved ordinary entry A at sequence s, then approve exit E at s after the witness stalls. The old witness can settle A while the exit key settles E. Two admissible settlements freeze the group. The owners need not approve a fresh malicious action after choosing to exit. Receipt expiry limits an honest witness's window, but a hostile witness can backdate a receipt within the allowed interval; signatures do not prove time. Waiting out expiry is not a Byzantine escape proof.

A witness cannot unilaterally change the roster or invent a valid competing entry. It can censor, and with competing approved candidates it can permanently freeze the group under the current rules. A frozen group has no specified resolution path; replacing the witness after the fork cannot erase the conflict. State what clients must persist when they detect conflict, whether all new grant uses stop, and what restoration/restart does. Persisting only the highest head is not a persistent freeze record.

An old witness can also sign a fresh nonce head for an old prefix after its replacement. A first-time or rolled-back verifier derives the witness from that prefix and cannot learn the replacement from the XID alone. This needs an explicit trust assumption or an additional currentness anchor; genesis authentication does not solve it. The same issue occurs with a dishonest current witness hiding revocation. Key separation alone does not establish independent operators or honest clocks.

**Satisfaction:** state the trusted-witness/clock/storage assumptions and the conditional exit guarantee; specify durable freeze and post-freeze behavior, outgoing versus incoming head signer at migration, and the old-witness rollback limit. Add exit-vs-ordinary-entry, two exits, restart-after-freeze, and old-witness nonce scenarios. If Byzantine non-capture is required, provide a different predeclared ordering/recovery protocol; otherwise explicitly accept permanent unavailability in these cases.

### R7 — The lockout guard ignores the signature budget

**Classification:** safety/availability defect.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Lockout guard”, “Acceptance”, fixed limits; delta role/key and limits requirements.

A group with 64 owners and `all of owner` can be valid. A policy change to `roster = 1 of owner`, `policy = all of member` can name a role held by 65 existing members (admitted earlier as nongoverning members). It passes the role-holder lockout check and can be approved under the old policy within 64 signatures, but the resulting policy can never be satisfied by a v1 entry. Similarly an otherwise authorized roster expansion under `majority` can cross into a required threshold of 65. This is structural impossibility, not loss of a private key. Cross-role conjunctions and required incoming signatures also compete for the same budget.

**Satisfaction:** define what satisfiability the guard promises, validate effective rule thresholds against the evidence budget, and account for the joint roster guards and incoming signatures where the promise covers those transitions. Add an all-of-65 policy-change rejection and a majority crossing the cap. Explain that 256 roster members does not mean 256 governing signatures are representable.

### R8 — Reconcile the broader governance contract, not only decision 1

**Classification:** contract mismatch with ADR-013's referenced living governance specification.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Status and boundaries”, “Identifier”, “Acceptance”, “Exit witness”; `proposal.md`, “Impact”; delta scope requirements.

The core amendments owed extend beyond “Stable collective XID”. “Collective control evidence” requires an identity document, binding proof and authorizing keyspace checkpoint. “Handoff phases are independently abortable” explicitly requires owner and settlement-backend replacement to use prepare/authorize/effect/activate. This design has neither that binding model nor that phase machine; policy replacement activates in one settled entry, with no incoming witness readiness. No secret/control-key effect exists in this v1, so collapsing phases may be a reasonable restricted profile. It still needs an explicit applicability/compatibility argument, not a claim to implement the whole existing contract. Also preserve the conceptual separation between collective identity and a replaceable shared-key lineage, even though the founding group chain anchors identity permanently.

**Satisfaction:** provide a concise requirement-by-requirement compatibility map: implemented, intentionally restricted, deferred, or requiring core amendment. Include identity/control evidence, handoff phases, migration readiness, consumption and freshness, not just identifier derivation. Gate downstream reliance on those amendments/profile contracts. H2 may remain in the core projection work if this wire makes no unverified promise that existing XID-document consumers already accept it.

### R9 — Alternate receipt times change descendant validity

**Classification:** ambiguity with a safety/availability consequence.

**Applies:** `design.md`, “Settlement” and “Transport”; delta “A witness receipt settles an entry” and expiry requirements.

This finding was prompted by self-critique item 8, after the independent findings above were persisted. Suppose the same entry E has valid receipts at times 10 and 20, both within its expiry. Its child C has a receipt at 15. C passes monotonicity with the first receipt for E and fails with the second, although E's content hash and state are identical in both bundles. The at-most-one-*entry* rule does not prohibit signing the same entry twice. The conflict rule only covers different entries and therefore does not settle this disagreement. Normal receipt retries or witness/exit receipts on the same replacement entry need an explicit rule too.

**Satisfaction:** pin the receipt identity and retry semantics, and define how conflicting dates for one entry affect replay. Ensure a descendant commits to the relevant settlement evidence, or provide another deterministic rule that handles both honest retries and contradictory evidence without choosing by arrival order. Add the two-bundle example above as a conformance scenario. Merely excluding receipt time from State does not resolve descendant validity.

## Scenario and requirement coverage audit

No entire named requirement is scenario-free. That does **not** mean its normative clauses are tested. Missing negative cases include: unknown body keys and a second parent (linear chain); member/exit-key collision (roster); numeric zero, empty role, and absent revoke rule (authorization/guard); exact expiry equality (expiry); wrong receipt key/domain/group/hash and future receipt time (settlement); mixed exit/witness conflicts (exit); already-spent nonce and handoff racing install (head); wrong grant group, expired/not-yet-valid grant and excessive head age (grants); member-count/entry-byte limits, unknown auth/release and visibility (limits). A verifier could violate each named clause while passing all current scenarios.

Design rules absent or incompletely represented as requirements include the exact body/Policy/State/Grant/LogBundle shapes, sorted state arrays, roster set/drop reducer, unmatched-signature non-invalidation, the `group.policy` acceptance exception, zero-nonce semantics, the witness's durable at-most-one signing obligation, and the assertion that a settled entry/state permits light-client restart. R4–R6 must turn these into precise normative rules and scenarios. In particular, either withdraw the light-client restart claim or define the authenticated trust anchor and historical data it needs.

## Non-blocking notes

- Retain delegate-first and no group signing key: they avoid a large, unnecessary key-custody protocol here.
- No wire HTTP endpoint is needed; deployment discovery can remain separate from evidence verification.
- Public member XIDs remain linkable. A later pseudonymous profile needs a versioned semantic distinction, not just reuse of a 32-byte field.
- The packet calls keyspace §12 “governance”; the current file's §12 is “Out of scope”. I read the actual standard and did not infer nonexistent governance provisions.
- H1 remains unverified experimentally; H2 is explicitly deferred. The run workflow should spike applicable hypotheses before act. This review did not execute product tests or modify implementation.
