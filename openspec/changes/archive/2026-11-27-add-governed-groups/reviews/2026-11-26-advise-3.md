## Required change

### T1 — State the clock assumption that makes the staleness bound true

**Classification:** contract mismatch (overstated safety bound), with an age-threshold ambiguity.

**Files/sections:** `design.md`, “Head and freshness,” “What a head does not give,” and “Grants”; `specs/identikey-group/spec.md`, “Head freshness and no rollback” and “A grant is delegation under bounded staleness.”

The amendment states a bound of `head_age + 60` assuming the witness's clock is right. The future-head check does not establish that the verifier's clock is within 60 seconds of that clock. It only compares the verifier's present clock with one signed timestamp, which may be old.

Counterexample, with an honest witness and clocks that advance normally at the same rate:

- At real/witness time 1000, the witness signs a head containing a grant with `head_age = 60`. Its validity window is broad enough to include all times below.
- At real/witness time 1001, the witness settles revocation.
- At real/witness time 10000, a first-contact verifier whose clock reads 1050 receives the old head, with no nonce requested and no later head known.
- The signed time 1000 is not in the verifier's future. Computed age is 50. The grant passes the stated freshness and validity-window checks, although revocation occurred 8999 real seconds ago.

No witness lie, clock jump, or conflicting settlement is needed. A stale signed head cannot prove the current clock offset. The existing “Head slightly ahead” scenario proves a 120-second acceptance interval from that particular observation, assuming normally advancing clocks; it does not bound how long ago the head was actually issued for every possible first observation.

**What satisfies T1:** explicitly condition the real-time bound on a suitably synchronized verifier clock at each authorization check, as well as an honest, current witness with a correct clock. For the stated upper bound, verifier time must not lag real/witness time by more than 60 seconds. Requiring absolute skew at most 60 seconds is a simple sufficient assumption. State that checking a head's timestamp does not establish this assumption, and that without it v1 supplies no finite real-time staleness guarantee. No new synchronization protocol is requested.

With `h` the honestly issued head time, `v` verifier time, `t` true time, and `H = head_age`, the current age predicate gives `v <= h + H`. The missing assumption `t - v <= 60` then gives `t - h <= H + 60`. This proves the intended bound for a revocation after that head, under the already disclosed honest-currentness assumption.

Also change “when its head is older than the bound” in “What a head does not give” to refer to the actual acceptance predicate: computed age greater than `head_age` fails. The `+60` is a real-time uncertainty allowance, not another 60 seconds added to the computed-age threshold. As written, that paragraph's “Otherwise” can permit computed age 90 with `head_age = 60`, while “Grants” forbids it.

Carry the assumptions into the delta and add a scenario distinguishing an unboundedly slow verifier clock from an admissible skew, plus a computed-age boundary verdict. Keep the existing future-at-tolerance and policy/revocation scenarios. This is a clarification of the chosen bounded-staleness architecture, not a demand for ordered consumption or a new witness mechanism.

## Consistency checks and steelman

**Signature counts.** Let `O` be the union of prior roster keys and `A` be the resulting roster keys minus `O`. Every supplied key is in `O ∪ A`, supplied keys are unique, and all of `A` sign. Thus a non-founding presentation has exactly `|signers ∩ O|` old signatures and `|A|` added signatures, limited to 64 and 16 respectively. Removing an old key does not move its approval to the added bucket. A role-acceptance signature by a retained old key still occupies the old bucket once. A newly added key does not become a prior-state approval merely because its member XID already exists: the explicit prior-key approval rule still applies. Founding uses its separate all-keys-sign rule and 64-key cap. Founding at 16, 17, and 64 keys passes these limits; 65 fails. Other guards still apply. The missing dedicated 16-key scenario is non-blocking.

**Canonical replay.** Closed maps, canonical dCBOR, sorted unique arrays, and required-versus-optional array rules now determine one encoding for a given State/body value. Empty `State.grants` cannot also be omitted; an empty `keep` cannot also be present. Identical policy with fewer kept grants is a real transition; identical policy with all grants kept fails. Different intentionally chosen policies or actions that happen to yield equivalent authority are not competing encodings of one value. No new state-encoding contradiction found.

**Root authority.** After founding, only roster and policy entries can change the root rule, its holders, or their keys. Policy entries require the prior policy rule; relevant roster entries require it through the role/key guard. Delegate and revoke do not alter those fields. Founding establishes its own root under its explicit founding rule. The new authorized-transfer claim therefore matches the entry kinds and formula thresholds.

**Freshness scenarios.** Knowing a later head or failing computed freshness rejects an old bundle. Lacking later knowledge while still meeting freshness permits either the pre-revocation or pre-policy-change grant. These four verdicts agree. The remaining problem is the claimed real-time guarantee and the explanatory use of “bound,” not their local verdicts.

**Anchor.** The revised section expressly scopes its guarantees to a future profile and admits withheld siblings. The earlier freeze sentence “The anchor is that way” is read as a forward reference to those future requirements, not as a v1 guarantee. The delta now explicitly accepts stale first contact before adoption. Known adoption must remain fail-closed; a writer should preserve that knowledge rather than assume a previously accepted pre-adoption head records it. This is implementation guidance for the stated no-further-authorization rule, not a request to specify the anchor now.

The strongest reading of the contract answers the blind take: prior-state authority, universal possession evidence, canonical standing-grant replay, and honest limits on witness trust are all deliberate and workable. It would be easy to assume an adequately synchronized verifier clock in that reading. T1 asks for that assumption to be written because the current text names only the witness clock and advertises a concrete safety bound. Missing vectors, scenario breadth, and H1 do not motivate this verdict.
