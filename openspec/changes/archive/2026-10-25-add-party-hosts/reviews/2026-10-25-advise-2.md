## Blind take — persisted before opening design, tasks, or the previous review

1. Pin each party hostname to one exact WebAuthn rp.id and an operator-approved origin; refuse ceremonies and issuance for unknown or suspended hosts.
2. Preserve discoverable, sync-capable access passkeys; hostname partitioning must not silently require enclave custody or platform authenticators.
3. Treat successful WebAuthn as an access gate only; refuse its use as an IdentiKey Response or as satisfaction of the OIDC URN grant.
4. Bind enrollment to possession of the precise certificate subject key, with fresh, single-use, audience-bound evidence tied to the party and group.
5. Pin a single dual-certificate binding and issuance order so neither representation can be paired with an unrelated credential or circular hash dependency.
6. Specify signed bytes, field types, algorithms, fingerprints, and canonical encodings sufficiently for independent implementations to agree.
7. Pin the X.509 purpose, key usages, constraints, extension identifiers and criticality, and mapping to dCBOR; refuse accidental TLS-server or CA authority.
8. Bound verification to the configured local-group trust anchor and explicit v1 path shapes; reserve and reject boundary presence without inferring global trust.
9. Distinguish HTTPS entry and public discovery from authenticated group entry, identifying which proof authorizes each operation and what suspension invalidates.
10. Accept the operational cost of per-party hostnames and dual encodings in exchange for clear credential grouping, provided trust remains explicit and locally scoped.

## R2 follow-up — High: define the expected subject in the enrollment transaction

Evidence: `design.md:180-218`; `specs/identikey-party/spec.md:135-152`.

The nonce is bound to party id, rp.id, origin, and credential id. The acceptance list compares those fields to the ceremony session, but checks the subject only against the key that verifies the submitted signature. That establishes possession of the submitted key; it does not establish equality with the key authorized for this enrollment. The delta explicitly requires a valid signature under K2 to fail when the session was authorized for K1, yet the design never records K1 or compares against it.

Concrete disagreement: on the first submission of a fresh nonce for credential C, a caller supplies subject K2 and a valid K2 signature over every required field. An implementation following the acceptance list can accept. An implementation following the K1/K2 scenario must reject. The later second-submission rule cannot resolve this first-submission case.

Specify where the authenticated holder selects or authorizes the subject key and where the endpoint pins that expected key in the pending enrollment. Require submitted `subject` to equal that stored selection as well as verify its signature. The selection may occur after passkey registration; no change to the WebAuthn ceremony is required. Also make the nonce expiry server-controlled explicitly: store its deadline and reject a holder-supplied `exp` that extends it. The current phrase “nonce ... unexpired” is best read that way, but the signed map alone must not become the expiry authority.

Acceptance evidence: a successful first submission under the pinned K1; rejection of a first, otherwise-valid K2 submission; rejection of an extended expiry; and the already specified replay, cross-session credential, and suspended-party failures. Fixture hooks suffice for this standards-only change.

## R1 follow-up — Medium: reconcile validity encoding with the referenced X.509 profile

Evidence: `design.md:101-108,280-281`; `specs/identikey-party/spec.md:173-178`.

Both amended files require GeneralizedTime for all holder validity dates. RFC 5280 requires conforming issuers to use UTCTime through 2049 and GeneralizedTime from 2050 onward; verifiers must process both. Thus this is an issuance-profile defect, not a claim that every X.509 library rejects GeneralizedTime. See [RFC 5280 §4.1.2.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5280.html#section-4.1.2.5).

Prefer the RFC date-dependent encoding rule while retaining whole-second UTC values and semantic equality with `nbf` and `exp`. The paired hash already commits to the actual DER, so it does not require a single ASN.1 time tag. If a deliberately non-PKIX issuance profile is intended instead, state that exception and its interoperability contract explicitly rather than leaving the standard's RFC relationship implicit.

Acceptance evidence: hooks covering a present-day certificate and the 2049/2050 transition, with identical Unix-second meanings in the dCBOR twin and no change to the C0 → X → C construction.
