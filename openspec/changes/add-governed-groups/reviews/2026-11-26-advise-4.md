## Arithmetic and scenario consistency

Write `h` for the head timestamp, `v` for verifier time, `t` for real time, and `H` for the grant's maximum head age. The operative rules are:

- Reject when `h > v + 60`.
- Compute `A = max(0, v - h)`.
- Require `A <= H`, without adding 60 to H.

Since H is positive, acceptance implies `v <= h + H`. With verifier time within 60 seconds of real time, `t <= v + 60`, hence `t - h <= H + 60`. An honest witness with a correct clock makes h the real issuance time of a then-current head. For a revocation or policy change after that head, its elapsed age cannot exceed this head-age bound while the old head still passes. Honesty must cover the issuance being relied on: a newly honest witness does not retroactively authenticate a previously dishonest currentness assertion. This is the ordinary meaning of the stated witness trust assumption.

All three conditions matter. A dishonest witness can freshly sign an old prefix; an incorrect witness clock can misdate it; a slow verifier can treat a genuinely old timestamp as fresh. The future check establishes none of those trust assumptions.

| Scenario or boundary | Check |
|---|---|
| Computed age at the limit | H=60 accepts A=60 and rejects A=61. A=90 and A=120 also fail; there is no extra computed-age allowance. |
| Head slightly ahead | h=v+60 is allowed and A=0. After 121 seconds of normal clock advance, A=61 and it fails. A head more than 60 seconds ahead, including the one-hour case, fails immediately. |
| Verifier clock within tolerance | h=1000 and H=60 imply v<=1060 and t<=1120. At t=1120 the worst permitted lag gives v=1060 and acceptance at equality; later real times cannot pass under the assumption. |
| Verifier clock runs slow | At t=10000, v=1050, h=1000 gives A=50 and passes the timestamp tests. The verifier violates the synchronization assumption, so the 8999-second-old revocation is no counterexample to the conditional guarantee. |
| Revocation or policy change inside the bound | A 30-second-old head may still show the grant standing. Learning a head that includes revocation or removal ends acceptance immediately. A policy change that keeps the grant leaves it standing. |
| Old bundle | Known later state prevents rollback independently of clock age. Without that knowledge, failing computed freshness refuses authorization; passing it permits the explicitly bounded stale view. |
| Witness replacement and multiple sources | Known replacement or a longer valid chain defeats an old-prefix head. A head over the replacement must use the incoming witness. A fresh old-prefix head on first contact remains the disclosed witness-currentness exposure, not a guarantee supplied by the clock test. |
| Nonce and durable action | A wrong nonce fails; a matching nonce does not order the subsequent action against revocation. Durable binding and handoff-race scenarios remain outside this standard's authority. |
| Window and replay | Grant validity-window checks remain additional conditions. Replay reads no clock, so different clocks produce the same standing-grant state, though live-use verdicts can differ. |

These scenarios assume other validity checks pass when isolating their named condition. In particular, the slow-clock and future-at-tolerance examples do not override grant windows, nonce checks, stored conflicts, or known later state.

## Unsupported entries and steelman

The Log amendment now requires remembering that the group became unsupported and continuing to refuse authorization when later shown a shorter prefix. Anchor explicitly records the XID and unsupported sequence as durably as the highest head, rather than relying on the pre-adoption head to encode that knowledge. The delta adds the shorter-log scenario. This answers the pass-3 implementation note without claiming that first-contact verifiers can discover a withheld adoption.

The strongest case against my blind take is that this deliberately small trusted-witness protocol cannot provide universal currentness or durable-action finality. It need not: those are explicitly excluded, and the accepted tradeoff is delegation with a conditional real-time stale-authorization limit. The now-explicit verifier-clock assumption is sufficient; no clock synchronization protocol or ordered consume mechanism must be invented to write this standard.

No new operational contradiction was introduced by the amendment. The exact future-tolerance predicate, computed-age threshold, and scenarios determine the verifier behavior consistently.
