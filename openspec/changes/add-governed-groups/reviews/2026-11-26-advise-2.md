## Independent blind take

1. Pin genesis authentication separately from currentness; a founding commitment cannot certify the latest head.
2. Honor the owner's bounded-staleness choice and refuse any implication of durable installation authority.
3. Honor policy-root authority while checking whether its advertised self-protection survives threshold changes and removals.
4. Require proof of possession for every introduced key, with precise treatment of keys moved between principals.
5. Count prior-state approvals and incoming acceptance separately without letting transport limits strand advertised operations.
6. Require a deterministic reducer, including policy equality, empty arrays, and grant reissuance.
7. Pin the wrapped Gordian digest and wire signature association independently of the actor-only log verifier.
8. Treat witness trust, permanent freeze, and stale first contact as explicit availability and currentness assumptions.
9. Require receipt evidence choices to leave descendant validity unchanged for the same authenticated chain.
10. Trade stronger recovery and consumption for a small trusted-witness profile only with honest core-contract boundaries.

## Required changes

### S1 — State the root invariant as authorization of change, not immutability of eligible coalitions

**Classification:** contract mismatch (false safety claim).

**Files/sections:** `design.md`, “The policy rule is the root” and “Role and key guard”; delta requirement “The policy rule is the root.”

The sentence “No sequence of valid entries” can give policy power to a set that previously lacked it is false. With owners A and B and `policy = 2 of owner`, both may approve a policy entry lowering it to 1. A alone now passes although A alone did not before. This is an intended, authorized transfer of power.

Formula thresholds and drops give the same counterexample without changing policy: start with owners A, B, C, `policy = majority of owner`, `roster = majority of owner`. A and B sign a roster entry dropping B and C. All affected rules pass against the old state, the resulting one-owner majority passes the lockout guard, and A can now govern alone. No incoming consent is needed for a drop. The literal invariant nevertheless forbids the result.

**Satisfaction:** say that changing the policy rule, or changing its eligible principals/keys through a roster entry, requires satisfaction of the *prior* policy rule. Explicitly allow that authorized change to empower a previously insufficient coalition. Add the lowering and majority-drop scenarios with their intended verdicts. Preserve the owner's policy-root decision; no stronger mechanism is requested.

### S2 — Make empty-state encoding and no-op scope consistent

**Classification:** ambiguity with incompatible canonical state encodings.

**Files/sections:** `design.md`, “Reducer” and `State` shape; delta “Replay is a total function of the entries.”

The shape rules say “A present empty array fails” for every body and `State`. But `State.grants` is mandatory and founding has no grants. Thus the literal rule rejects every founding state. It also rejects the result of revoking the last grant or voiding all grants. An implementer who assumes the sentence means only *optional* arrays instead accepts those states and hashes `grants: []`.

The no-op rule also refers to the prior state for “an entry,” including genesis, which has no prior state. For non-founding entries, the canonical ordering and closed shapes otherwise make equality straightforward. In particular, a policy entry with an identical policy but a reduced `keep` set changes state and presently passes; one preserving both policy and all standing grants fails. That distinction should be intentional and covered.

**Satisfaction:** explicitly allow required empty `State.grants`, limit empty-array rejection to the intended fields, and scope prior-state equality to non-founding entries. Add scenarios for founding with empty grants, revoking the last grant, and identical-policy entries with unchanged versus reduced grants. Clarify that per-member identical `set` rejection applies even when another member changes, as the current design already says. No new history store is needed.

### S3 — Separate founding and subsequent admission budgets explicitly

**Classification:** ambiguity.

**Files/sections:** `design.md`, fixed limits and “Acceptance”; delta “Mechanisms and limits fail closed.”

The general rule says one entry adds at most 16 keys, each signing; the next rule allows a founding roster of 64 keys, all signing. A 32-key founding entry adds 32 keys to a previously nonexistent roster. One reading rejects it under the general rule; another treats the founding sentence as an implicit exception. The delta uses SHALL for both without stating the exception.

Also pin whether “adds” means a key absent from the entire prior roster or a new `(member XID, key)` association. An entry can remove a key from A and assign those same bytes to B while maintaining uniqueness in the resulting roster. Its signature is attributable to A for prior-state approval, while it may also be B's possession evidence. Implementers should not choose whether that signature occupies the 64 bucket, the 16 bucket, or both.

**Satisfaction:** explicitly exempt founding from the post-founding 16-key limit; define old and added key sets mathematically and count each signature once for transport limits. Either forbid cross-principal reassignment in one entry or define its approval attribution, acceptance, and budget treatment. Include founding at 16/17/64/65 keys and a key-reassignment scenario. This does not require raising any limit.

The narrower lockout promise itself is acceptable. At most 48 distinct principals can supply one key each within the 64-old-key budget. Joint requirements may exceed 64, and the design now says so. A policy quorum can structurally repair that configuration with a policy-only entry. This is not a promise that every roster batch, or a group whose signing keys have been lost, remains operable.

### S4 — Reconcile freshness scenarios and the actual time bound

**Classification:** contract mismatch and ambiguity.

**Files/sections:** `design.md`, “Head and freshness” / “What a head does not give”; delta “Head freshness and no rollback,” especially “Old bundle still verifies,” and “A grant is delegation under bounded staleness.”

“Old bundle still verifies” says a pre-policy-change bundle used after the change must be refused. But the new bounded-staleness scenario deliberately accepts a still-fresh pre-revocation head. A policy change voiding the grant is explicitly subject to the same bound. A verifier that has not learned the change cannot implement both verdicts.

The allowed future timestamp also changes the advertised duration. At verifier time 1000 a head dated 1300 is accepted. With `head_age = 60`, at verifier time 1350 that head is still only 50 seconds old by the specified subtraction. A revocation just after 1000 has then been hidden for roughly 350 seconds, not 60. This can arise from the permitted clock offset, without witness equivocation. The age is negative initially; unsigned wire timestamps do not define how a verifier should handle that arithmetic.

**Satisfaction:** qualify the old-bundle rejection by knowledge of the later state, failed freshness, or a stronger downstream profile. State the accepted clock-skew assumptions and effective staleness bound, or tighten the head validity rule to deliver the advertised bound. Define future-but-within-tolerance age handling without unsigned underflow. Add policy-handoff-within-bound and future-head-at-the-tolerance scenarios. Keep the explicit prohibition on durable installs.

### S5 — Qualify the reserved anchor's claims and migration visibility

**Classification:** false safety claim / contract mismatch with the disclosed trust model.

**Files/sections:** `design.md`, “Anchor,” “What the witness is trusted for,” and “Freeze”; delta anchor-adoption scenario if its preconditions are expanded.

The slot and fail-closed behavior are sound. But “An anchored group is never checked at the weaker level by mistake” is not true for a first-contact verifier shown only the valid prefix before anchor adoption plus a fresh head from the witness named there. The wire already expressly permits that verifier to accept the stale prefix. A v1 verifier stops only when it actually encounters the adoption entry; the slot does not force that discovery.

Likewise, a store described only as holding at most one hash per sequence does not make every conflicting settlement visible. A second approved sibling and its receipt may be withheld from the store. A canonical store may define which entry is authoritative instead, but that is a future finality rule, not visibility of both settlements. “Removes the three things v1 leaves to trust” also needs qualification: the choice of store, its currentness, availability, and verification assumptions are still unspecified.

**Satisfaction:** describe these as requirements for the future anchor profile, not established guarantees of a reserved slot. Limit v1 downgrade prevention to verifiers that have authenticated adoption (and retain it), and carry forward the stale-first-contact limitation until the future profile supplies an independent discovery/currentness trust anchor. Distinguish canonical selection from conflict publication. Do not invent the future store's bytes in this change.

## Independent checks and steelman

The strongest reading of the design is a deliberately modest trusted-witness protocol. Under that reading most original objections are answered rather than postponed. Policy-root authority is simpler than independently entrenched powers. Universal key signatures make later policy-induced promotion safe with respect to possession, without requiring new consent. Standing grants and explicit reissuance avoid tombstones and clock-dependent replay. A permanent freeze is a legitimate disclosed failure mode, and the present spec no longer promises a Byzantine escape.

The five changes above are needed even on that favorable reading: they reconcile normative text and guarantees rather than replace the architecture. S1 is not evidence of an unauthorized takeover; it is an overstrong invariant contradicted by intended authorized transitions. S3 does not demand that all multi-rule operations fit. S5 does not require an anchor now.

The key-per-signature transport is safe when every supplied pair is verified and candidate membership is checked. Swapping keys or adding an unattributable signature makes that presentation invalid; it cannot create a new approved entry or a freeze. Gordian orders signature assertions by digest, so `keys` must be paired after encoding/reading the actual wire order, never by `counter_sign` insertion order. `op.rs` explicitly documents this distinction.

The corrected sign digest agrees with `Author::raw_sign`: `to_envelope(op).wrap().digest()`. `codec::content_hash` instead computes BLAKE3 of canonical unsigned envelope bytes. The standard writer should spell out the core map, the Gordian leaf/wrapper digest construction, signature tag/layout, and assertion digest order, or normatively pin the corresponding format; a floating Rust API name alone is not portable documentation. Those facts are determined by the cited implementation, not a new design choice. H1 should also ensure the group validator enforces the profile itself: `codec::from_envelope` reads known core fields without checking the core map's entire key set, and extracts signature subjects. Successful generic decoding alone does not establish the exact closed envelope shape this design requires. Canonical round-trip equality or equivalent structural checks can enforce it.

For the same valid chain and different individually admissible receipts, R9's change removes the previous replay divergence. Invalid or expired evidence can still produce pending/reject while valid evidence settles; that is not disagreement between two valid proofs. A verifier missing a conflicting sibling can continue while one possessing it freezes, as the trust section now explicitly admits. Different fresh heads and clocks can also produce different *action* decisions within the accepted staleness model; they must not change replayed state.

- The map's readiness sentence should say an exit key can leave an unresponsive new witness **if configured and usable**, with the already stated no-competing-candidate condition. It is optional.
- The `State.grants` schema comment still says “live grant digests”; the reducer correctly says standing. Align the comment when making S2.
- The proposal's shorthand “two receipts at one sequence” should say two settlements of **different entries**; repeated receipts of one entry are now harmless.
