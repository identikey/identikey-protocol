## Blind take

Written before opening design.md or tasks.md, from the proposal's Why, the living draft `docs/standards/identikey-group-v1.md`, and cited `crates/identikey-log/tests/cosign.rs`. The living draft already incorporates the founding clarification; this is independent of the change design, not independent of all authored specification text.

1. Pin the group XID to the founding entry's bytes.
2. Require consent from every founding key.
3. Define founding authorization without a prior state.
4. Count members once, regardless of their key count.
5. Keep later approvals tied to keys held before the entry.
6. Refuse a new key's attempt to approve its own admission.
7. Apply lockout checks at founding as well as later.
8. Reconcile founding key limits with quorum limits.
9. Distinguish authenticated unreadable evidence from invalid rivals and verified conflicts.
10. Accept the availability tradeoff of stopping on authenticated unsupported evolution, provided unauthenticated evidence cannot cause that stop.

## Comparison and steelman

Founding cannot use prior-state authority: its roster and policy are committed by the configured XID, and all roster keys must consent. Its own-roster counting rule is therefore necessary, not a weakening of the subsequent admission guard. Likewise, an unreadable transition cannot be fully validated by a v1 verifier; requiring full validation before recording unsupported would defeat the authenticated upgrade path. Prior-root approval and the in-office witness authenticate the decision to leave what v1 understands. These are justified exceptions with explicit boundaries, and answer the blind concerns without requiring a different design.

## V1: resolved

Design “Signatures” now directs founding to its own counting rule; “Authorization: quorum(k, role)” defines that rule; “Acceptance” explicitly scopes the added-key exclusion to entries after founding. The delta's “Quorum authorization per action” and “Every key signs the entry that adds it” make the same exception normative. Every founding key remains an added key and must sign, but each member holding the policy role counts only once toward the founding policy rule.

The lockout guard remains unconditional: every resulting rule must have a holder, numeric thresholds must be positive and reachable, and no rule may require more than 48 members. The founding explanation's phrase about the number of holders does not replace those enumerated checks. In particular, all signatures being present does not legalize a zero threshold, an empty governing role, or a rule needing 49 members.

The founding budget is separate from that threshold guard: at most 64 roster keys, all sign, no other signature, with the existing per-member and signed-byte limits. Sixty-four signing keys do not mean a rule may require 64 members. A 64-key founding can have, for example, 64 one-key owners with majority rules (33 needed), or fewer members with several keys. An all-of-64 rule remains rejected. Multiple keys never multiply one member's vote.

The founding scenarios agree when read with the mandatory validity checks:

- One owner with one key and 1-of-owner rules succeeds.
- Three one-key owners, all signed, with policy 2-of-owner succeeds.
- A 2-of-owner founding with only one listed owner signing fails; a single-holder roster with that threshold also fails the lockout guard.
- Naming three members but obtaining signatures from only two fails even if those two could satisfy policy. Every key, including additional keys on a signing member's seat, must sign.
- The 17-key and 64-key founding scenarios are valid budget examples for otherwise valid entries; 65 keys fails. Neither successful scenario exempts its policy from the lockout guard.
- The identifier scenarios still bind a valid founding to its XID; a different founding has a different XID. Empty initial grants are required and do not invoke the post-founding no-op rejection. Unknown founding mechanisms remain rejected.

For later entries, the exclusion remains exact. Adding key X to owner A's seat does not let X exercise A's existing role. With 2-of-owner rules, B's old key plus X produces only B's approval and fails; an old key of A is still needed. Old-key consent to a promotion may also count as approval for roles already held. The founding exception does not extend to either case.

## Unreadable rival: consistent

Design “Transport” now explicitly routes an unreadable rival through “Unreadable entries,” rather than the ordinary full-validation/ignore path. Establishment requires the accepted predecessor and next sequence, verified signatures without duplicate keys, a signing old-key proposer, the prior policy quorum from old keys, and the in-office witness receipt. An established unreadable rival causes persistent unsupported status and denies new action, whether received before or after a readable sibling. Failure to establish it creates no durable finding.

This is not a second validated settlement record: expiry, transition semantics, and resulting state have not been validated. Thus it does not itself satisfy the freeze predicate. Two different fully validated settlement records at one sequence still cause permanent conflict. Nor does the new sentence clear an already stored conflict: the freeze rule still requires conflict to be reported afterward whatever bundle arrives. The delta's establishment and permanent-freeze requirements agree with this distinction.
