## Blind take

Written before opening design.md or tasks.md, from the proposal's Why, existing Forgejo/encoding standards, and crates/identikey-log/tests/cosign.rs.

1. Pin the group identifier to authenticated founding bytes, with no transport-supplied authority shortcut.
2. Pin every approval to identical entry bytes and resolve keys to distinct principals in prior state.
3. Refuse ambiguous signature attribution, malformed keys, duplicate encodings, and hidden assertions.
4. Require proof of possession for added keys without allowing them to bootstrap ordinary prior-state authority.
5. Pin a deterministic replay result independent of delivery order, wall-clock time, and detached payload availability.
6. Refuse unauthenticated evidence that can durably poison a group's verifier state.
7. Pin freshness to the actual authorization state and preserve observed progress across later presentations.
8. Refuse witness-exit authority broad enough to change membership or revive/revoke unrelated permissions.
9. Distinguish bounded delegation staleness from durable installation, which needs an ordered consume protocol.
10. Accept reduced availability under authenticated equivocation or unsupported upgrades as a tradeoff for refusing uncertain authority.

## Comparison and disposition

The amendments answer the blind concerns about unauthenticated durable
poisoning, heads detached from the authorization state, grant-preserving
exit, and canonical keys. The strongest argument for the design is that
a v1 verifier need not understand a future transition to authenticate
the prior root's decision to leave v1. Requiring a full v1 replay of that
transition would defeat the upgrade path. Equally, a head can be useful
evidence against rollback even when it cannot authorize an action now.
Those distinctions are sound. The remaining issue is a contradictory
founding rule, not a request for a competing design.

### P1: authentication, parsing, and rivals

An attacker cannot manufacture an established entry without obtaining
both the prior policy quorum's signatures and the in-office witness's
receipt. Extra keys cannot help reach that quorum; the proposer must
also be an old signing key. A presenter can relay already authenticated
upgrade evidence without possessing those private keys, which is
intended. Stripping or corrupting that evidence can leave another
verifier uninformed, just as withholding any later entry can; this is
not a promise of global agreement across different evidence sets.

Reading mechanism/visibility values before the closed-map check does
not require a permissive CBOR decoder. Canonical body encoding and a
well-formed signed envelope still apply. Unknown map keys are deferred
semantic validation, not permission for duplicate keys, ambiguous
encoding, or elided signed material. The specific unreadable branch
must govern the generic known-kind/body and candidate-key rules; the
design expressly allows verified non-old keys without counting them.
The predecessor and next sequence remain checked even though the
unrecognized transition, its expiry, and its resulting state cannot
be interpreted.

The cost is accurately stated: a future kind approved below `policy`
does not establish permanent unsupported status. It can be rejected as
evidence, with an unseen-prefix verifier still relying on freshness.
The honest-current-witness assumption remains necessary. A deliberate
upgrade that needs old verifiers to stop must be authenticated by the
old root and old witness.

An established unreadable rival does have an outcome: the design
explicitly admits it via `rival`, and the specific rule stores and
reports unsupported. It must not be treated as an ordinary invalid
rival and silently discarded. Nor does establishment alone prove a
fully valid second settlement record: the verifier has not checked
expiry, the transition, or the state digest. Thus the generic
two-validated-settlements freeze rule need not turn that case into
conflict. Both ordinary invalid rivals and readable valid competing
settlements retain their stated outcomes.

### P2 and P6: freshness is not settlement ordering

The three new head scenarios follow: a head behind a known revocation
cannot authorize use; a head past the available chain is rejected;
and an authenticated old head at 9 prevents a later retreat to 7.
Retention does not waive the future-time bound, signer selection,
entry-hash check, or a challenge the verifier actually issued. A
lower-sequence head does not replace the stored highest head.

The two-source and retired-witness scenarios agree with the longest
held chain rule, even if the witness has not changed. The old-prefix
staleness cases remain applicable when the later entries have not
reached the verifier. Once it holds the later chain, the older head
cannot authorize use even without a fresh head for the later entry.
The existing computed-age boundary and clock assumptions are
unchanged. None of this orders a later revocation against an action
after a successful check; "What a head does not give" still applies.

### P4: exit is a complete state comparison

For nonempty standing grants, an exit replacement must supply `keep`
equal to the entire prior grant set. With no grants, omitting `keep`
preserves the empty set; a present empty optional array still fails.
Dropping a grant yields a potentially valid ordinary policy entry but
an inadmissible exit receipt, hence pending with that receipt alone.
Naming the exit key as witness instead fails resulting-state validity.
Two otherwise admissible exits, or an admissible exit racing a settled
ordinary entry, still freeze. Neither the new predicate nor `keep`
clears a stored freeze.

### P5 and P7: exact bytes and scope

The public-key definition matches the canonical point-decoding
requirements of [RFC 8032 §5.1.3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8032.html#section-5.1.3).
The explicit small-order exclusion and uncofactored, encoded-R
comparison define the stricter profile here. "Not small order" does
not mean "prime-subgroup only"; the amendment does not silently impose
that different rule. It covers roster, proposer/supplied signing,
witness, and exit keys rather than only keys exercised by a signature
in this bundle. The noncanonical-witness scenario follows directly.

Grant top-level closure does not close profile-defined `holder` and
`scope` interiors. Canonical decoding/re-encoding prevents hashing a
normalized substitute for the presented body. Stored findings deny
grant use independently of whether the digest remains standing in the
historical state. Both new grant scenarios follow from the SHALL text.

## Required change

### V1 — Make the founding exception explicit in the added-key rule

**Classification:** blocking normative contradiction / interoperability.

**Files and sections:** `design.md`, "Authorization: quorum(k, role)"
and "Acceptance"; `specs/identikey-group/spec.md`, "Quorum authorization
per action" and "Every key signs the entry that adds it".

The delta says every founding key is added, then says an added-key
signature SHALL NOT count toward **any** rule and only an old-key
signature can count. It also requires founding to meet its own policy
rule. The design repeats the absolute exclusion while saying that all
founding signatures meet that rule. For a one-owner, one-key founding
entry with a 1-of-owner policy, the founding requirement needs one
counted signature and the new rule permits zero. The same problem
affects the 17-key and 64-key successful founding scenarios.

The strongest charitable reading is that the own-roster founding rule
is intended as an exception. That is almost certainly the intended
behavior, but an implementer should not have to override an explicit
SHALL NOT appearing in the very paragraph that classifies founding
keys as added.

**What satisfies this review:** scope the no-added-key-approval and
old-key-only counting rules to entries after founding, and expressly
state how founding signatures satisfy its own policy against its own
roster. Keep the requirement that every founding key signs, and keep
the later-entry exclusion even for a new key added to an existing
owner's seat. Reconcile both contract files and give an explicit
successful founding example alongside the existing missing-signature
rejection. Filled cryptographic vectors are not required for this fix.

## Recommendations to the user

**P1: agree with the author's recommendation.** A receipt alone would
let the witness fabricate a permanent unsupported finding without
root approval. Never retaining unknown-kind evidence would allow
rollback after an authenticated upgrade. Prior-root approval plus the
in-office witness is the appropriate boundary, with the documented
cost for lesser-rule future kinds.

**P4: agree with the author's recommendation.** The exit path should
settle replacement of the witness while preserving the rest of the
state. Owners can subsequently revoke grants under the normal rules
and new witness. Grant cancellation during exit would widen the
exceptional settlement path beyond its stated purpose. This is a
recommendation, not the user's confirmation; the ASK box stays open.
